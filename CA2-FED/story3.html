East Germany ⇆ West Germany

The Fall of the Berlin Wall

After 28 years of division, strangers from both sides climbed onto the wall together and started tearing it down with their bare hands.

Quick Facts

When
9 November 1989
Where
Berlin, Germany
Who
East and West Berliners, on both sides of the wall
Remembered for
Crowds climbing the wall together and chiseling it apart, piece by piece

The Berlin Wall had split the city in two since 1961 — concrete and barbed wire cutting through streets, separating families, guarded by soldiers under orders to shoot anyone trying to cross. For 28 years, it was the clearest physical line the Cold War ever drew.

On the evening of 9 November 1989, an East German official misspoke at a press conference, announcing that new travel rules would take effect "immediately" — when he'd meant to say the next day. Confused crowds began gathering at the checkpoints anyway, and by nightfall, overwhelmed and unauthorized, the border guards simply opened the gates.

People who had never been allowed to know each other spent that night side by side on top of the thing that had kept them apart.

What followed wasn't organized by any government. East and West Berliners climbed onto the wall together, strangers hugging and passing bottles of champagne up to one another. Within hours, people arrived with hammers and chisels and started breaking the wall apart themselves, taking pieces home as souvenirs of the thing that no longer stood between them.

The official demolition of the wall took months, but the real ending happened that first night — in the noise of a crowd that had waited 28 years to stand in the same place at the same time, and decided not to wait for permission any longer.
