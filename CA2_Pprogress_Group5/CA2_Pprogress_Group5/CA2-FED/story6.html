Chile ⇆ The World

The Rescue of the 33

When 33 miners were trapped half a mile underground, engineers and specialists from a dozen countries raced together to bring them home.

Quick Facts

When
5 August – 13 October 2010
Where
San José mine, Atacama Desert, Chile
Who
33 Chilean miners & an international rescue effort
Remembered for
The Fénix capsule rescue, watched live by roughly a billion people

On 5 August 2010, a collapse sealed 33 men inside the San José copper-gold mine, nearly half a mile beneath Chile's Atacama Desert. For 17 days, no one on the surface knew if any of them were still alive.

Then a drill bit broke through to the shelter where the miners had been rationing scraps of food, and came back up with a note taped to it: "Estamos bien en el refugio los 33" — "We are well in the shelter, the 33 of us." What had been a national tragedy turned into an international effort almost overnight. Chilean engineers were joined by specialists from NASA, drilling teams and equipment from the US, and mining experts from across South Africa, Australia, and beyond — racing to design and drill a rescue shaft before the miners' physical and mental health gave out.

For 69 days, a mine collapse in the Atacama Desert became the one thing the whole world was watching together.

The rescue itself took almost 24 hours. One at a time, each miner was pulled to the surface inside a narrow capsule named Fénix 2 — Phoenix — and the entire operation was broadcast live, watched by an estimated one billion people across the planet.

None of the engineers who designed that rescue shaft had worked together before that August. They came from different companies, different governments, different continents, and took orders in different languages — brought together by nothing but the same 33 names and the same clock.
